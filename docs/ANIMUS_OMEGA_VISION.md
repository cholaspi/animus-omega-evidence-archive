# Animus Omega: Vision and Proposed Applications

**Status:** Vision statement and proposed architecture

**Date:** September 14, 2026

**Evidence status:** Not an experiment, formal specification, or amendment to prior evidence

## Purpose

Animus Omega investigates whether cyclic structures can help persistent, model-informed simulations preserve consequential history as their worlds develop.

The long-term vision is a living archive: a simulated world in which different inhabitants can hold different perspectives, consequential choices remain traceable, and divergent represented histories may reconnect only through declared continuity and reconciliation rules.

This vision motivates the research. It does not establish that the proposed system works.

## The research begins with a narrower question

The minimum **Animus cycle** asks whether a bounded computational history can satisfy reciprocal closure:

1. execution from a declared beginning produces an ending;
2. the ending produces a return condition;
3. that return is required to construct the next beginning; and
4. the resulting history passes independently stated validation checks.

This minimum contract does not require branching, information loss, consciousness, physical time travel, or a claim about the structure of the universe.

The optional **Animus Omega Cycle** is a stronger proposed architecture. It adds declared represented branches and deterministic reconciliation before the ordinary reciprocal return is applied. Branch reconvergence, information loss, continuity through loss, and reciprocal closure remain separate outcomes.

## Proposed long-term capabilities

A mature implementation could study whether a persistent simulated world can support:

- **Consequential memory:** important actions, relationships, obligations, and unresolved events remain available under explicit retention rules.
- **Bounded perspectives:** inhabitants receive defined observations rather than unrestricted access to the private global state.
- **Auditable divergence:** represented branches have declared origins, identities, and provenance.
- **Constrained reconciliation:** branches merge only under a frozen equivalence rule, with conflicts and discarded distinctions recorded.
- **Validated return:** an ending-derived condition is checked against the requirements of the next beginning.
- **Discoverable continuity:** inhabitants may encounter permitted records or consequences of earlier events without receiving administrator-level knowledge.

These are proposed capabilities. Each requires an operational definition, executable implementation, matched controls, and evaluation.

## Important distinctions

### Shared understanding is not branch reconvergence

Inhabitants reaching a common intention is a coordination outcome. Multiple represented branches reducing to one authoritative history is a reconciliation outcome. An ending-derived return satisfying the beginning is a closure outcome.

The outcomes may interact, but one does not establish another.

### Temporal access is not unrestricted history editing

A declared visit to an earlier point in a candidate or authoritative history is different from restoring a discarded branch. Under the current proposed reconvergence rule, discarded branches cannot remain as hidden executable copies for later revival.

Any architecture that permits restoration or new branching must declare a new origin and specify how identity, provenance, and authority are handled.

### Memory must respect the observer contract

An inhabitant may receive breadcrumbs, records, inherited facts, or other permitted observations. That does not imply access to the complete ledger, validator, or hidden global state.

If a study changes what an observer can access, that change requires an explicit specification or fixture revision.

### Population is not a closure criterion

Population growth or contraction may be part of a particular simulated scenario. It is not a universal requirement for reciprocal closure, branch reconciliation, continuity, or computational efficiency.

### Narrative and philosophical language is not scientific evidence

Stories and philosophical interpretations can motivate questions and offer experiential ways to understand them. Operational research must still identify the corresponding records, permissions, transitions, validation rules, and measurable outcomes.

The program does not treat consciousness, measurable emotional frequencies, physical retrocausality, or the reality of a fictional world as established prerequisites.

## Development direction

Work toward the larger vision should proceed through bounded, separately reviewable stages:

1. Define the observer, retention, and inheritance contracts.
2. Implement a small persistent world with explicit records and access rules.
3. Define branch origins, protected equivalence, conflicts, and reconciliation.
4. Add reciprocal closure only after the underlying state and provenance rules are inspectable.
5. Compare the combined architecture with strong conventional baselines.

These are development stages, not numbered experimental results. New mechanics require their own specification and evaluation.
